# Communication and Influence

Senior engineers make complex work understandable enough for people to coordinate, make decisions, and act. Good communication is a technical tool: it reveals assumptions, exposes disagreement early, reduces rework, and preserves context beyond a meeting.

## Write for the decision or action

Start with the audience's question. A decision brief can state the problem, desired outcome, options, recommendation, tradeoffs, risks, and requested decision. A design note can describe context, constraints, interfaces, failure behavior, migration, and operational ownership. A status update can state progress against the outcome, changed assumptions, risks, next action, and owner.

Use diagrams when they make relationships, flows, or boundaries easier to inspect. Pair them with enough text to explain semantics and consequences. Keep a decision record when future contributors will need to understand why the team chose a path.

## Adapt detail without changing the facts

For an engineering peer, discuss interfaces, data contracts, failure modes, and evidence. For a product or business partner, connect choices to user workflow, scope, timing, cost, and risk. For an executive, foreground outcome, options, confidence, dependencies, and the decision needed. Offer technical depth as needed, but do not hide uncertainty behind jargon or false precision.

## Listen and investigate disagreement

Restate the other person's concern before answering. Ask what evidence or constraint would change their view. Separate disagreement about goals from disagreement about methods or assumptions. Use examples, prototypes, data, and explicit tradeoffs to resolve uncertainty. If the team still disagrees, name the decision owner and how the decision will be revisited.

## Influence without relying on title

Build credibility by understanding others' incentives, following through, making work easier, giving credit, and raising risks early with options. Seek alignment before a decision becomes urgent. Escalate with a concise account of impact and a recommendation, not only a problem. Managing up means sharing context and risks early while retaining ownership of actions within your control.

## Feedback and review

Make reviews specific, respectful, and tied to agreed goals or engineering constraints. Distinguish blocking correctness or safety issues from preferences. Explain the reasoning, invite alternatives, and notice useful contributions. In sensitive conversations, describe observable behavior and its effect, then agree on a next step and follow up privately.
