## Evidence discipline

The most recent cloud transformation program concluded before production release, and the CV says production outcomes were not measured. Describe the architecture, leadership, design decisions, stakeholder work, and delivery capabilities accurately. Do not present projected value as realized production impact. A strong next example would include post-release evidence: adoption, throughput, error rates, cycle time, cost, reliability, or another outcome tied to the original need.

## Development direction: from delivery to business outcomes

Build on stakeholder and architecture experience by practicing a consistent discovery-to-outcome loop:

- Gather examples of the underlying problem and validate who experiences it.
- Map the current workflow, data, decision points, workarounds, and exception paths.
- Establish baseline evidence and agree what a successful future state means.
- Compare process change, configuration, conventional software, and agentic automation.
- Make cost, risk, user adoption, operational ownership, and reversibility part of the tradeoff.
- Deliver a thin slice with observable acceptance criteria and a plan to measure value.
- Return after release to compare results with the baseline and decide what to change next.

## Development direction: from technical lead to capability builder

Choose deliberate practice goals that extend current strengths:

1. **Business analysis:** lead a discovery brief from ambiguous request to validated need, options, decision, and success measures.
2. **Commercial reasoning:** state expected value, build/run/support cost, risk exposure, and the basis for prioritization.
3. **Agentic workflow design:** create bounded tasks, repository context, test/evaluation harnesses, permissions, and human approval rules for agents.
4. **Outcome measurement:** define baseline metrics before delivery and report actual post-release impact, including when a hypothesis fails.
5. **Team growth:** delegate ownership, coach through review, and create feedback loops that make the team less dependent on a single technical lead.
6. **Executive communication:** present tradeoffs in terms of user impact, risk, cost, options, and recommendation, with technical detail available when needed.
