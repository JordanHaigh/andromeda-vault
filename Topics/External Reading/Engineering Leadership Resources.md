# Engineering Leadership Resources

A curated entry point for the four user-supplied repositories. The vault's practice guides synthesize themes from these lists and roadmaps into locally navigable material; this page points to the original maintainers for the full and changing collections. Resource lists are starting points, not a required curriculum.

## The supplied repositories

- [Resources to become a great engineering leader](https://github.com/gregorojstersek/resources-to-become-a-great-engineering-leader) — broad reading across system design, leadership and management, senior engineering, product thinking, business, AI, articles, newsletters, books, and people. The README recommends choosing a few resources based on a retrospective of current strengths and gaps. Start with its sections on system design, leadership, engineering, and becoming product-minded.
- [Senior Developer Roadmap](https://github.com/glennsantos/senior-developer-roadmap/) — an explicitly alpha, work-in-progress roadmap organized around code quality, communication, decision making, leadership, and project planning. It suggests marking existing strengths, then choosing gaps for further study.
- [Tech Lead Roadmap](https://github.com/glennsantos/tech-lead-roadmap) — an explicitly alpha roadmap intended to follow the senior developer map. Use it as a prompt for a development conversation, not as a universal role specification.
- [Awesome Technical Leadership and Career Resources](https://github.com/Jareechang/awesome-technical-leadership-and-career-resources) — a categorized reading list covering leadership, writing, career growth, ongoing learning, expectations, and scaling teams. Its references include the Etsy engineering career ladder and Tech Lead Expectations guidelines.

## A useful study sequence

1. Map current evidence with [[Topics/Business Analysis/Leadership Evidence and Development]] and [[Topics/Senior Engineering/Index]].
2. Choose one gap that matters to the next role or current team's outcomes.
3. Read one relevant item from a source list, then apply the idea to live work.
4. Agree scope and decision rights with the manager, team, or stakeholders involved.
5. Capture behavior and impact, request feedback, and review the result.
6. Update the development goal when new evidence shows the next constraint.

## Notes on using the roadmaps

The roadmaps are authored resources with their own scope and may change. The Senior Developer and Tech Lead repositories label their maps as alpha or works in progress. Team expectations differ by company, product, and level; use current role criteria and feedback to interpret the material. For career conversations, prefer concrete examples and outcomes over completing a checklist.
