# Technical Leadership

A tech lead creates conditions for a group to solve the right problem and deliver a coherent, supportable result. The role commonly combines hands-on technical work with direction, coordination, communication, and coaching. It does not require being the team's only decision-maker or strongest individual contributor in every specialty.

Technical leadership is distinct from line management, though roles can overlap. A tech lead is accountable for technical coherence and delivery support within an agreed scope; a manager is accountable for people management and organizational responsibilities. Clarify expectations and decision rights with the team.

## Operating cadence

**Before work:** confirm the problem, affected users, expected outcome, scope, constraints, decision owner, and baseline. Identify technical unknowns and dependencies. Make a thin plan with sequence, parallel work boundaries, checkpoints, and risk responses.

**During work:** keep design and domain context accessible; unblock decisions; communicate changes early; review interfaces and integration points; make progress visible through working increments. Delegate ownership with context and authority, not only a list of tasks.

**Before release:** verify acceptance evidence, security and operational readiness, migration and rollback, support ownership, and communications. Keep release authority and approvals explicit.

**After release:** inspect service and user outcomes, support load, incidents, and feedback. Compare with the baseline. Close follow-up actions and share learning. Adjust the plan when evidence changes.

## Healthy leadership signals

- Decisions have owners and are made at the lowest responsible level.
- Risks, dependencies, and uncertainty are visible early enough to act.
- People can explain the outcome and why their work matters.
- Review and delegation help others gain context and ownership.
- The team can respond to production behavior without depending on one person.
- Technical standards improve safety and flow without becoming ceremony for its own sake.
- Success is judged by user and system outcomes, not lines of code, ticket counts, or agent output volume.
