# People Leadership Beyond Mentoring

Mentoring is one part of helping a team succeed. Technical leads also influence the conditions in which people do their work: expectations, feedback, collaboration, workload, learning, decision ownership, and continuity. The exact boundary with line management varies, so make responsibilities explicit and work with the manager rather than quietly taking on formal people decisions without authority.

## Give useful feedback

Base feedback on an observed action and its effect, not a label about someone's character. Describe the context, what happened, the impact on users or the team, and the change needed. Ask for the person's view and listen for missing context. Agree a specific next step and check back. Deliver time-sensitive feedback soon; handle sensitive performance matters privately and involve the people manager where policy, fairness, or formal process requires it.

Positive feedback should also be concrete. Explain which choice, behavior, or contribution helped and why it mattered. Credit the person who did the work rather than absorbing the accomplishment into the lead's story.

## Work through conflict

When disagreement persists, identify whether it concerns the goal, facts, constraints, ownership, or implementation choice. Give each person time to explain the underlying concern. Summarize the points of agreement, name the unresolved decision, and agree who owns it and what evidence would help. If behavior is harming safety or trust, address it directly and promptly; do not disguise a conduct problem as a technical debate.

## Delegate for growth and delivery

Delegate a meaningful outcome with its context, boundaries, decision authority, support, check-in cadence, and escalation conditions. Match autonomy to the person's experience and the consequence of failure. Avoid assigning only the implementation while retaining every decision for yourself. Stay available, ask what support would help, and let the person own the work unless a risk or changed constraint requires intervention.

Review the outcome and the person's learning together. Ask whether the scope was clear, the authority sufficient, the checkpoints useful, and which part they want to own next time.

## Protect sustainable workload

Observe interruption load, after-hours work, blocked tasks, uneven operational duties, and whether urgent requests repeatedly displace planned work. Make capacity and tradeoffs visible. Rotate support responsibilities fairly where possible, create cover for critical knowledge, and distinguish a short-term incident response from a chronic staffing or priority problem. A tech lead can surface and mitigate work-system issues but should coordinate with the manager on accommodations, performance, compensation, or formal wellbeing support.

## Hire and onboard for team capability

Work with the hiring manager to describe the actual work and competencies needed rather than seeking a copy of the current lead. Use consistent job-related criteria, structured questions, and evidence from work samples that are fair, accessible, and proportionate. Avoid relying on unpaid production-like work or culture-fit impressions.

An onboarding plan should give newcomers a map of the product, domain vocabulary, architecture, development environment, support model, security expectations, decision records, and people to ask. Assign an onboarding partner and a first scoped contribution. Check access, setup, and clarity early; revise the process based on where newcomers get stuck.

## Build continuity and new leaders

Find critical knowledge that sits with one person and make a risk-based plan to spread it through pairing, documentation, exercises, ownership rotation, and succession. Do not rotate specialist work mechanically if it adds risk; build backup capability deliberately. Create opportunities for others to facilitate a design review, incident review, stakeholder discussion, or release with appropriate support.

## Measure team health carefully

Use team-level feedback and delivery signals to find systemic constraints, not to rank individuals. Useful indicators include review delay, on-call distribution, interrupted work, onboarding time to first change, recurring dependency waits, and whether people feel able to raise risk. Interpret quantitative signals with private feedback and context. Do not turn one proxy into a performance target that changes the behavior it was meant to observe.

## A one-to-one or coaching prompt set

- What work feels most valuable right now, and why?
- Where are you waiting, repeating work, or missing context?
- What decision would you like to own next?
- What feedback would help you make progress?
- Is workload sustainable, including support and interruptions?
- What should I change as a lead to help the team work better?
- What did we agree to follow up on, and when?
