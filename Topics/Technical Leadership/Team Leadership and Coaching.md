# Team Leadership and Coaching

Technical leadership is expressed through the capability of the team. A lead builds trust, shares context, grows judgment, and makes ownership broader. Helpful leadership does not turn the lead into the permanent router for every decision or the only person who can safely change a critical system.

## Build trust through daily behavior

Be clear about goals and constraints. Follow through or renegotiate openly. Admit uncertainty and correct mistakes. Give credit to contributors and address problems directly without humiliation. Invite concerns from people closest to the work, including operations and support. Treat production failures as a chance to understand system conditions and improve safeguards, while retaining accountability for follow-up.

## Mentor through real work

Ask what the engineer is trying to learn and how much direction they want. Explain the reasoning behind feedback. Pair on difficult work, model investigation, and gradually transfer responsibility. Give feedback soon, privately when sensitive, and based on observed behavior and impact. Ask the learner to propose the next step; confirm understanding rather than assuming agreement.

## Delegate outcomes with support

A clear delegation agreement names the outcome, boundaries, constraints, decision authority, available support, check-in points, and escalation conditions. Match autonomy to experience and consequence. Stay reachable without taking the task back at the first disagreement. Review results and learning, not only whether the person followed the original plan.

## Improve reviews and shared practice

Review for correctness, user behavior, domain rules, security, operability, maintainability, and evidence. Separate required changes from optional preferences; explain the reason. Keep review latency low enough to sustain flow. Turn recurring issues into shared examples, automated checks, documentation, or design discussion instead of repeating the same comments.

## Grow shared ownership

Rotate incident roles and operational knowledge where practical. Pair people across boundaries. Keep runbooks and architecture notes current. Identify single-person dependencies and make a gradual plan to spread context. Create opportunities for engineers to lead a design, stakeholder conversation, release, or retrospective with support appropriate to the risk.

## Team health and performance

Notice workload, interruptions, unclear priorities, conflict, and burnout signals. Coordinate with the people manager when issues need formal support. Use team measures to find friction and learning opportunities, not to rank individuals or incentivize shallow output. Track whether interventions improve safety, quality, flow, and sustainability.
